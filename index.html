📋 Etiology Deep Dives

Septic Shock (62% of all shock)

Definition (Sepsis-3): Sepsis with persisting hypotension requiring vasopressors for MAP ≥65 mmHg AND lactate >2 mmol/L despite adequate volume resuscitation. [1][5]

Common Sources:

  • Pulmonary — pneumonia (most common), empyema
  • Abdominal — peritonitis, cholangitis, abscess, ischemic bowel
  • Urinary — pyelonephritis, obstructed infected system
  • Skin/Soft tissue — necrotizing fasciitis, cellulitis
  • Line-related — CLABSI

Management: Early broad-spectrum antibiotics (within 1hr) + source control → IV crystalloid → norepinephrine → vasopressin → hydrocortisone if refractory. [5][2]

Anaphylactic Shock

Pathophysiology: IgE-mediated mast cell degranulation → massive histamine release → vasodilation + bronchospasm + capillary leak + mucosal edema. [5]

Clinical Features: Rapid onset after exposure, urticaria/angioedema (~90%), bronchospasm/stridor, hypotension, GI symptoms.

Common Triggers: Antibiotics, NSAIDs, NMBAs, foods, insect stings, latex, contrast, blood products.

Management: STOP offending agent → IM Epinephrine 0.3–0.5mg (repeat q5–15min) → aggressive IV fluids → secure airway → adjuncts (H1/H2 blockers, steroids, albuterol). [5]

⚠️ Epinephrine is the ONLY first-line treatment. Do NOT substitute antihistamines or steroids.
Neurogenic Shock

Pathophysiology: Disruption of descending sympathetic pathways (cervical/high thoracic SCI, T6 and above) → loss of vascular tone + loss of cardiac accelerator fibers → vasodilation + bradycardia. [5]

Distinguishing Features: Hypotension + bradycardia (unlike all other shock types); warm, flushed skin below level; poikilothermia.

Management: Spinal stabilization → Target MAP 85–90 mmHg (spinal cord perfusion) → Norepinephrine or phenylephrine → Atropine for symptomatic bradycardia. [5]

⚠️ Rule out hemorrhage. Trauma patients may have concurrent hemorrhagic shock — do not attribute all hypotension to neurogenic cause.
Adrenal Crisis

Pathophysiology: Inadequate cortisol response to stress → impaired vascular tone + decreased catecholamine sensitivity. Most common ICU cause: chronic steroid suppression. [2][5]

Clues: Steroid history, refractory hypotension despite fluids + vasopressors, hyponatremia, hyperkalemia, hypoglycemia, eosinophilia.

Management: IV Hydrocortisone 200mg/day (50mg q6h). Often dramatic improvement in vasopressor responsiveness within hours. [2][5]

Post-CPB Vasoplegia

Pathophysiology: Contact activation with bypass circuit → systemic inflammatory response → NO-mediated vasoplegia. Incidence 5–25% post-CPB. [9]

Management: Norepinephrine → Vasopressin → Methylene blue (inhibits NO/guanylate cyclase) → Hydroxocobalamin (NO scavenger). [9]

Drug-Induced Distributive Shock

Culprits: Calcium channel blockers, sedatives/anesthetics (propofol, volatiles), vasodilators (nitroprusside, NTG), ACEi/ARB, cytokine release syndrome (post CAR-T).

Key Principle: Identify and discontinue offending agent. Supportive vasopressor therapy. For CCB OD: high-dose insulin, IV calcium, lipid emulsion.

🔬 Pathophysiology of Distributive Shock

The primary defect is in the periphery — inappropriate vasodilation with maldistribution of blood flow, decreased SVR, and impaired oxygen utilization despite preserved or elevated cardiac output. [1]

Vasodilatory Mechanism
Proinflammatory mediators enter systemic circulation → vasoplegia and organ dysfunction. The main deficit: decreased SVR and altered oxygen extraction. [1][2]
Nitric Oxide (NO) Pathway
Inflammatory signaling upregulates iNOS → excessive NO → activates guanylate cyclase → smooth muscle relaxation. Also: acidosis opens K-ATP channels → membrane hyperpolarization → catecholamine resistance. [5]
Cytokine Cascade
TNF-α, IL-1, IL-6 drive endothelial activation, capillary leak, microcirculatory dysfunction, and coagulation activation. [2]
Relative Hypovolemia
Vasodilation increases venous capacitance → blood pools peripherally → effective circulating volume decreases without absolute fluid loss → ↓ venous return. [1][6]
Capillary Leak & Glycocalyx Injury
Inflammatory mediators damage endothelial glycocalyx and tight junctions → plasma extravasation → interstitial edema → ↓ effective volume + impaired O₂ diffusion. [1][5]
Microcirculatory Dysfunction
↓ Capillary density, ↓ perfused capillaries, ↑ flow heterogeneity. Blood "shunts" past exchange vessels. Persistence predicts poor outcomes independent of macrovascular correction. [4]
Myocardial Depression
Circulating depressant factors (TNF-α, IL-1β, NO) → biventricular dilation with reduced EF. Often reversible. Contributes to "cold"/late septic shock phenotype. [5]

Early vs. Late Septic Shock

Early ("Warm"): Hyperdynamic — ↑CO, ↓↓SVR, warm extremities, bounding pulses, wide pulse pressure. SvO₂ elevated (impaired extraction). [5][6]
Late ("Cold"): Myocardial depression → ↓CO, mottled skin, poor cap refill, narrow pulse pressure. Worse prognosis. [5]
⚠️ Pearl: "Warm" + low SVR + preserved CO = distributive. "Cold" + low CO may be cardiogenic, hypovolemic, OR late distributive — echo differentiates.

💊 Medical & Pharmacologic Management

Initial Resuscitation

Volume Resuscitation
IV crystalloid 30 mL/kg within first 3 hours (SSC). Individualize — assess fluid responsiveness before additional boluses. Excess fluid worsens outcomes. [4][5]
Source Control
Drain abscesses, debride necrotic tissue, remove infected devices, repair perforations. Every hour of delay matters. [5]
Antimicrobials
Empiric broad-spectrum within 1 hour. Cultures first if possible without delaying. De-escalate on results. [5][2]

Escalation Pathway [2][5]

👆 Mark current step:

STEP 1Norepinephrine (First-Line)
"We consider norepinephrine the vasopressor of first choice" [1]. Target MAP ≥65 mmHg. [4]
STEP 2Vasopressin (0.01–0.04 U/min)
Non-catecholamine; V1 receptors. Catecholamine-sparing. Delay associated with ~20% ↑ mortality per 10 mcg/min NE. [5]
⚠️ Do not exceed 0.04 U/min. Do not titrate to MAP. [1]
STEP 3Adjuncts: AngII / Epinephrine / Hydrocortisone
AngII: ATHOS-3 [7]. Epi: Refractory/inotropy needed [1]. Hydrocortisone 200mg/d: If ≥2 vasopressors or escalating doses. [2][5]
Methylene Blue: Refractory vasoplegia (post-CPB, late septic). [9]
↓ DE-ESCALATIONWeaning Vasopressors
Phase 4: Wean adjuncts first → vasopressin last (or keep fixed until NE low) → NE last. Target negative fluid balance. Signal: MAP at target on stable/↓ doses, lactate normalizing, UOP improving. [1][4]

🔍 Assessment & Monitoring

Continuous feedback loop: assess → intervene → reassess → adjust. [1][4]

🕐 Initial Assessment (First 1–3 Hours)

Hemodynamic Stabilization
MAP ≥65 mmHg (higher if chronic HTN). At what vasopressor dose? What is the trajectory? [4]
Volume Status & Fluid Responsiveness
After initial 30 mL/kg: assess responsiveness via PLR (spontaneous breathing), PPV/SVV (ventilated, regular rhythm), or mini-fluid challenge. If responsive → more fluid. If not → vasopressors. [4]
Source Identification
Cultures before antibiotics (if obtainable within minutes). Imaging as needed. Assess surgical source control needs. [5]

🫀 Perfusion Windows [1][4]

🧠 Neurologic
Confusion, agitation, obtundation. Often earliest sign. Assess RASS, CAM-ICU. [1]
🩸 Renal
UOP ≥0.5 mL/kg/hr target. Oliguria = early hypoperfusion. Rising Cr = established AKI. Trend > single value. [6]
🖐️ Cutaneous
CRT target ≤3 sec (ANDROMEDA-SHOCK). Mottling score 0–5. Cool extremities in "warm" shock = transition to cold phenotype. [4]

🧪 Metabolic Markers

Lactate
Target ≥20% decrease q2h until normalized. Persistence >2 after 24h = poor prognosis. Serial trending > single values. [4]
ScvO₂
Low (<65%) = inadequate O₂ delivery. High (>70%) in sepsis may = impaired extraction (NOT reassuring). [4]
Pv-aCO₂ Gap
>6 mmHg = inadequate CO relative to demand, even if ScvO₂ normal. Optimize flow. [4]

🫁 Echocardiography

Bedside Echo/POCUS
First-line imaging. In distributive: hyperdynamic LV, small cavity, IVC variability. Red flags for other Dx: dilated poorly-contracting LV (cardiogenic), RV dilation (PE), pericardial effusion (tamponade). [4]

💊 Vasopressor Responsiveness

NEE Trend
Escalating NEE = worsening. Decreasing NEE = resolving. Track as composite vasopressor burden metric. [7]
MAP/NEE Ratio
Higher = better (good MAP at low pressor cost). ≥325: ~20% mort | 136–324: ~35% | <136: ~55%. Validated in septic shock. [8]

🔄 Ongoing & Reassessment Triggers

Routine (q2–6h)
Lactate trend, UOP, vasopressor doses/NEE, perfusion windows, fluid balance, reassess fluid responsiveness before boluses. [4]
Escalate If
New/↑ vasopressor need, rising lactate, new organ dysfunction, failure to improve at 6–12h. Consider: inadequate source control? New infection? Bleeding? Adrenal insufficiency? Wrong shock type?
De-Resuscitation Signals
MAP at target on stable/↓ pressors, lactate normalizing, UOP adequate, no longer fluid responsive → target negative balance (Phase 4). [1][4]

🔬 Pathophysiology

Low CO due to primary cardiac dysfunction → sustained tissue hypoperfusion → systemic decompensation. [5][9]

Vicious Cycle
↓Contractility → ↓CO → ↓MAP → ↓Coronary perfusion → Further ischemia → Multi-organ dysfunction. [3][9]
Venous Congestion
Congestive organ damage (hepatic, renal) — ↑ mortality independent of CO. [3]
Mixed Shock (~20-25%)
Paradoxical vasodilation from inflammatory activation. PAC helpful. May need methylene blue or hydroxocobalamin. [9]
CPO = MAP × CO ÷ 451 — Strongest hemodynamic predictor of mortality (SHOCK trial). [3][9]

🔬 Pathophysiology

Physical impediment to blood flow. CO↓ due to mechanical obstruction. Rarest (~2%). [5][6]

Hemodynamics
↓CO, ↑SVR, ↑CVP, variable PCWP, ↓SvO₂, cool extremities. [5]
PE Mechanism
≥50% vascular bed → acute ↑RV afterload → RV dilation → septal shift → ↓LV filling → ↓CO. [5][6]

🔍 Etiologies & Treatment

Tension Pneumothorax

Dx: Clinical — absent lung sliding on POCUS. Tx: Needle decompression → thoracostomy. [5]

Cardiac Tamponade

Dx: Beck's triad, pulsus paradoxus. Echo: RV diastolic collapse. Tx: Pericardiocentesis. [5]

Massive PE

Dx: SBP<90 >15min. RV dilation, McConnell's sign. Tx: Thrombolysis, CDT, embolectomy, or VA-ECMO. [5][6]
