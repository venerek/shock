🔬 Pathophysiology of Distributive Shock

Distributive shock is characterized by inappropriate systemic vasodilation leading to maldistribution of blood flow, decreased SVR, and inadequate tissue oxygen utilization despite often preserved or elevated cardiac output. [1]

Vasodilatory Mechanism
Excess proinflammatory mediators and cytokines enter systemic circulation → vasoplegia and organ dysfunction. The main deficit lies in the periphery — decreased SVR and altered oxygen extraction. [1][2]
Nitric Oxide (NO) Pathway
Lactic acidosis impairs vascular tone via intracellular signaling and increased iNOS expression → decreased responsiveness to endogenous and exogenous catecholamines. [5]
Cytokine Cascade
In septic shock: TNF-α, IL-1, IL-6 drive endothelial activation, capillary leak, microcirculatory dysfunction. [2]
Relative Hypovolemia
Vasodilation + capillary leakage → relative intravascular volume depletion even without absolute fluid loss → ↓ venous return and preload. [1][6]
Capillary Leak
Inflammatory mediators damage endothelial integrity → plasma extravasation → interstitial edema → ↓ effective circulating volume + impaired O₂ diffusion. [1][5]
Microcirculatory Dysfunction
↓ Capillary density, ↓ perfused capillaries, ↑ heterogeneity of flow. Persistence associated with worse outcomes. [4]

Early vs. Late Septic Shock

Early ("Warm"): Hyperdynamic — ↑CO, ↓SVR, warm extremities, bounding pulses, wide pulse pressure. SvO₂ may be elevated (impaired extraction). [5][6]
Late ("Cold"): Myocardial depression → ↓CO, mottled skin, poor cap refill. Catecholamine-induced myocardial injury. [5]

🦠 Etiologies

Septic Shock (62% of all shock) [1]

Definition (Sepsis-3): Lactate >2 mmol/L AND hypotension requiring vasoconstrictors to maintain MAP >65 mmHg despite adequate volume resuscitation. [5]

Management: Early antibiotics + source control → IV fluids → norepinephrine → vasopressin → hydrocortisone if refractory. [5][2]

Anaphylactic Shock

Pathophysiology: Mast cell degranulation → histamine → massive vasodilation, bronchospasm, capillary leak. [5]

Management: STOP offending agent → IM Epinephrine 0.3–0.5mg → fluids → airway. [5]

Neurogenic Shock

Pathophysiology: Loss of descending sympathetic pathways → unopposed parasympathetic → vasodilation + bradycardia. [5]

Management: Surgical stabilization → MAP 80–90 mmHg → norepinephrine or phenylephrine. [5]

Adrenal Crisis

Management: IV Hydrocortisone 200mg/day — accelerates shock resolution, decreases vasopressor requirements. [2][5]

Escalation Pathway [2][5]

STEP 1Norepinephrine (First-Line)
"We consider norepinephrine the vasopressor of first choice" [1]. Target MAP ≥65 mmHg. [4]
STEP 2Vasopressin (0.01–0.04 U/min)
Non-catecholamine; V1 receptors. Catecholamine-sparing. Delay associated with ~20% ↑ mortality per 10 mcg/min NE. [5]
⚠️ Do not exceed 0.04 U/min. [1]
STEP 3Adjuncts: AngII / Epinephrine / Hydrocortisone
AngII: ATHOS-3 [7]. Epinephrine: Refractory/inotropy needed [1]. Hydrocortisone 200mg/d: Accelerates shock resolution. [2][5]
↓ DE-ESCALATIONWeaning Vasopressors
Phase 4: wean vasoactives, promote negative fluid balance. [1]

🔍 Assessment & Monitoring

Three "Windows" of Perfusion [1][4]

🧠 Neurologic
Altered mental state — obtundation, disorientation, confusion.
🩸 Renal
UOP <0.5 mL/kg/hr. Rising creatinine indicates AKI. [6]
🖐️ Cutaneous
CRT, skin temperature, mottling. CRT-guided resuscitation beneficial (ANDROMEDA-SHOCK). [4]

Biochemical Markers

Lactate
Target ≥20% decrease over 2hrs. Even small increases >2.0 associated with ↑ mortality. [4]
ScvO₂
Low (<65%) = inadequate delivery. Persistently high also poor (impaired extraction). [4]
Pv-aCO₂
>6 mmHg suggests persistent inadequate flow. [4]

Echo & Fluid Responsiveness

First-line imaging. After initial resuscitation, assess fluid responsiveness via PLR or PPV. [4]

Post-Sepsis Syndrome

Long-term Impairments

19% depression, 39% anxiety, 8% PTSD. 48% did not return to work. Only 33% returned to independent living by 6 months. [6]

🔬 Pathophysiology

A cardiac disorder resulting in clinical and biochemical evidence of sustained tissue hypoperfusion — low CO due to primary cardiac dysfunction → systemic decompensation. [5][9]

Vicious Cycle
↓Contractility → ↓CO → ↓MAP → ↓Coronary perfusion → Further ischemia → Multi-organ dysfunction. [3][9]
Venous Congestion
Congestive organ damage (hepatic, renal) — associated with ↑ mortality independent of CO. [3]
Mixed Shock (~20-25%)
Paradoxical vasodilation (↓SVR) from inflammatory activation. PAC especially helpful. May require methylene blue or hydroxocobalamin. [9]
CPO = MAP × CO ÷ 451 — Strongest hemodynamic predictor of mortality (SHOCK trial). [3][9]

AMI-CS: Worst prognosis (~30% in-hospital, ~59% 5-year). HF-CS: Better (~25% in-hospital). Post-Cardiotomy. Secondary: Arrhythmias, valvular, pericardial. [9]

💊 Management

VASOPRESSORSNorepinephrine (First-Line)
Target MAP ≥65 mmHg. [4][9]
INOTROPESDobutamine or Milrinone
No mortality difference (DOREMI). Target CI >2.2. Catecholamines may worsen long-term outcomes in HFrEF. [5]

Other: Judicious 250–500mL challenges · Diuresis if congested · Early revascularization (SHOCK trial) · Early tMCS if pharmacologic support inadequate. [3][5][9]

💪 Rehab

Ueno et al. 2025 — Early rehab for acute HF requiring IV inotropes: In-hospital mortality 9.9% vs 13.2% (p<0.001), shorter LOS, lower 30-day readmission. [6]

Key Principles

• Hemodynamic stability with routine care before progression
• PPV effects on CO variable — depends on preload/afterload/LV-RV dysfunction
• Conservative, response-dependent progression [5][6]

🔬 Pathophysiology

Loss of intravascular volume → ↓preload → ↓SV → ↓CO → tissue hypoperfusion. Hemodynamics: ↓CO, ↑SVR, ↓↓CVP, ↓PCWP, ↓SvO₂, cool/clammy. [1][5]

Hemorrhagic Cascade
Hypovolemia → reflex vasoconstriction → ↓organ blood flow → anaerobic metabolism → acidosis → endothelial dysfunction → glycocalyx shedding → coagulopathy. [5][6]

🔬 Pathophysiology

Physical impediment to blood flow. Volume and contractility preserved; CO↓ due to mechanical obstruction. Rarest (~2%). [5][6]

Hemodynamics
↓CO, ↑SVR, ↑CVP, variable PCWP, ↓SvO₂, cool extremities. [5]
PE Mechanism
≥50% vascular bed → acute ↑RV afterload → RV dilation → septal shift → ↓LV filling → ↓CO → collapse. [5][6]

🔍 Etiologies & Treatment

Tension Pneumothorax

Dx: Clinical — absent lung sliding on POCUS. Tx: Needle decompression → thoracostomy. [5]

Cardiac Tamponade

Dx: Beck's triad, pulsus paradoxus. Echo: RV diastolic collapse. Tx: Pericardiocentesis. Temporize: volume, epi boluses, minimize PEEP/VT. [5]

Massive PE

Dx: SBP<90 >15min. RV dilation, McConnell's sign. Tx: Systemic thrombolysis, CDT, embolectomy, or VA-ECMO. [5][6]
